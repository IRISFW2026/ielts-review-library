1127-写作-大作文-English-Medium Education 英语作为教学语言

正负面发展 · 目标 Band 7–7.5 · 40 分钟

考察要点 · 原题与考场作答指南

Original question · 原题

English is now widely used as the main language of education, especially in the education systems of some non-English speaking countries. Do you think it is a positive or negative development?

You should spend about 40 minutes on this task. Write at least 250 words.

正负面发展正负面发展

01 审题判断

题型：正负面发展

核心立场：总体积极，但前提是学生具备足够语言支持，且母语教育不被削弱。

审题关键词：总体积极，但前提是学生具备足够语言支持，且母语教育不被削弱。

02 解题思路与立意

  • 积极：接触国际学术资源；提升跨国升学和就业流动性；便于国际合作。
  • 消极风险：学生可能因语言负担而难以掌握学科内容；城乡和家庭资源差距扩大；本土语言文化边缘化。
  • 立场：有过渡课程、双语支持和母语保障时，整体利大于弊。

03 考场作答指南

  • 区分“英语授课”与“英语课”：前者会影响所有学科的理解。
  • 写出因果链：语言障碍→认知负担增加→学科成绩受影响。
  • 提出的条件要对应风险：预备语言课程、双语材料、教师培训。
  • 先用约 5 分钟完成审题与提纲，再按四段结构展开论证。
  • 每个主体段遵循“观点 → 解释 → 具体例子／结果”，避免只罗列观点。

04 四段式写作规划 · 写提纲

Introduction

→ 说明英语授课扩大的趋势，并给出有条件的积极立场。

Body 1

→ 解释英语作为学术通用语带来的资源获取、交流和发展机会。

Body 2

→ 承认语言门槛和教育不平等风险，提出语言支持与母语教育保障。

Conclusion

→ 重申积极性取决于实施方式，而非单纯改用英语。

每段只保留核心论点和论证方向；考场上据此扩展成完整段落。

Introduction

The use of English as the main language of instruction has expanded in many non-English-speaking countries. I regard this as a largely positive development, provided that schools offer sufficient language support and continue to value students’ first languages.

Body 1

One major advantage is that students can gain more direct access to international academic resources. A large amount of research, university-level material and professional communication is produced in English. Studying subjects such as science or business through English may therefore prepare learners for overseas education and international careers. It can also make cooperation between universities easier, as students from different countries are able to share ideas using a common language. These opportunities may be particularly valuable in fields where new knowledge is published and exchanged internationally.

Body 2

However, using English across the curriculum can create difficulties if students are not sufficiently proficient. They may spend so much effort understanding the language that they fail to grasp the subject itself. This problem is likely to affect children from families that cannot afford private language lessons more severely, widening educational inequality. A further concern is that local languages and cultural knowledge may receive less attention. Governments should therefore introduce English-medium education gradually, provide preparatory language classes and make bilingual learning materials available. Schools should also maintain strong teaching in the national language.

Conclusion

Overall, English-medium education can broaden academic and professional opportunities. Its benefits are most likely to outweigh the disadvantages when language support is accessible to all students and mother-tongue education remains part of the curriculum.
